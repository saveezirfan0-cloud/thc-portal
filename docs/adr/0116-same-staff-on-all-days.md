# ADR-0116 · Same staff on all days (slice 1: the requirement and who may be invited)

**Status:** Accepted for slice 1 · **Decisions marked PROVISIONAL await the client's answers** · Deliberately changes §3.2 · Builds on ADR-0043, ADR-0079, RULE-18, RULE-20 · Supersedes part of ADR-0043 (see Consequences) · **Request:** Sam Barrington-Wells (client's director), 12.10.2026

## Context

A client sometimes wants the SAME staff on every day of a multi-day job. The example in the request: Fri 20, Sat 21 and Sun 22 Nov, each "Bar Staff at Tobacco Dock (need to be available all 3 days)". Sam asked: "Is there a way to set this in the event build as a requirement?"

Today (§3.2): "Multi-day = separate events created via Duplicate (the clone copies the roles, NOT the staff)." Each day is its own event with its own date, its own `shift_requirements` rows and its own pool, filled independently. A worker can accept Friday and decline Saturday, and nothing in the build says the client cares.

## Decision (slice 1)

**§3.2 is changed deliberately.** "The clone copies the roles, NOT the staff" still holds: no booking, invitation or application ever travels with a Duplicate. What is new is a **requirement** on a role section that restricts who may be **invited**.

1. **Linking the days.** `events.series_id uuid null` groups the events that are days of one job. Saving a Duplicate links the clone to its source (`link_event_series()`) **only when the source event has at least one role with "Same staff on all days" switched on**; any other Duplicate ("copy last week's event") is only a copy and joins no series, so twenty of them never become "Day 7 of 20". The function sets `series_id` on **both** events: the source's own when it has one, else a new random uuid. It runs after the clone's event row and before its sections and first auto-assign round, so that round already sees the series. It refuses a different client (`createEvent` then carries on unlinked: duplicating into another client is only a copy of the roles), a clone already in another series, and a clone that is **not new**: it must have no bookings and have been created in the last hour, so a live, staffed event cannot be pulled into a series. There is no extra tick in the builder: switch the role on, save, then Duplicate to add the next day.
2. **The switch.** `shift_requirements.same_staff_all_days boolean not null default false`, **per role**, in the role section: "Same staff on all days" (a plain switch; violet stays Auto-Assign's). Off by default. Duplicate keeps it on the clone: it is the client's ask, not the day's. It is a **silent** edit (nobody booked is re-asked or removed) and, like `required_gender`, not under the §3.2 edit lock.
3. **The gate.** A worker is a candidate for a flagged section only if, for **every sibling section**, they pass what they would have to pass to be invited there. A sibling is a section in another event of the **same series and client**, the **same role**, with the flag **also on**, in an event that is **not cancelled**, a day that has **not yet ended**, and **starting within 14 days** of the section being judged (a series is a run of days, not a standing arrangement). The failures are one gate, **`not_available_all_days`**, reached by any of:
   - **a hard gate on that day** — the sibling's own pool gate: wrong role, blocked, booked elsewhere (confirmed or worked, with the 2 h different-venue gap and same-venue back-to-back fine), hours limit, right to work, self-cancelled off that event, do-not-return, staff gender and event language;
   - **the availability calendar** — a `staff_unavailability` entry over the sibling's window (ADR-0043);
   - **the projected hours** — see 4.

   The section's **own** gate always wins (the truer reason). `not_available_all_days` is the last. It joins `HARD_GATES`, shows under **Unavailable → Not available all days** on the event board (no "Invite anyway"), and has office copy for a refused manual invite and a refused application.
4. **Projected hours.** The section being judged and every sibling are grouped by the Mon–Sun cap week each **starts** in (Europe/London, `weekly_booked_hours`'s bucket) and their hours summed. The worker's other commitments in that week are counted **without** any of the series' own sections, so a day they already hold is counted once, as projected. Each week then goes through `rota_guard_decide()` — the decision `weekly_cap_would_breach()` already uses — so a Student visa band (20 h in term, 10 h below degree level), a work visa's own limit and a right-to-work stop always block, and a Working Time 48 blocks unless `rota_guard_mode` is `warn`. A signed 48-hour opt-out lifts the ceiling for a UK worker but not for a student visa in term time. Three 8 h days in one student-visa term week (24 h against 20 h) are refused although each day alone fits; two are not; five 9 h days (45 h) fit a UK worker until 4 h more in the same week makes 49; the same days either side of a Monday are judged a week at a time. This reuses the existing cap decision with projected hours; nothing falls back to "per day only". Pure twin: `projectSeriesWeeks()` + `seriesHoursBlocked()` in `packages/domain/src/series.ts`, held to `series.vectors.json`.
5. **Where it is enforced — two paths, because of the clock.** `authenticated` runs under `statement_timeout = 8 s` (20261012120000), and the pool is one scan of every worker (~1.5 s for 1,038 workers locally).
   - **One worker (`auto_assign_worker_gate`, `series_gate_for`).** `invite_worker`, `accept_invite`, `apply_to_shift`, `accept_application`, `take_offered_shift` and `staff_open_offers` judge ONE worker. They read **one row** of the section's own pool (`auto_assign_candidates_pool(section, escalation, array[worker])`, a new `p_only` filter that is the only clause added to the 20261008120000 pool body) and ask `series_gate_for(worker, section)`, which asks the same pool about that one worker on each sibling, then the calendar, then `series_hours_blocked`. No pool is scanned for any sibling; the gate expressions exist once, in the pool. Each of these functions is restated in the migration byte for byte from its latest body with the pool read as the only change (pgTAP 804 and the existing 130, 510, 550, 721, 767 pin their behaviour). `staff_open_shifts` (Radar) keeps its whole-pool read for RULE-17's wave check and adds `series_gate_for` for the viewing worker on a flagged section only.
   - **A list (`auto_assign_candidates`).** The event board and the auto-assign rounds read the wrapper over `auto_assign_candidates_pool()`: the section's pool, then **one pool run per sibling** over the workers the section's own gates left standing, then the projected hours per such worker. The sibling check asks the same question of another section, which is also why the section's own gates live in `auto_assign_candidates_pool()` (a wrapper that called itself would recurse).
6. **The event board** says "Day N of M" (live, by date, not counting cancelled days) in the Event panel's header actions beside the status pill, and a "Same staff on all days" pill on the role.
7. **The worker is told.** Accept, apply and take refuse with: "This job needs you free for every day of it." plus a line naming the causes and the office. (Not the "all 3 days" wording the client may want: that stays PROVISIONAL below.)

### PROVISIONAL — awaiting the client's answers (not built; designed so they can be added)

Recommended answers in brackets.

- **One acceptance booking all days.** Today Accept books one day. [Yes: accepting one flagged day offers all of them, as one action that confirms every sibling or none.] Needs a series-aware accept.
- **Cancel or remove from one day cascading to the others.** [Yes on a worker's self-cancel or an office withdrawal of a flagged day, with the office able to release one day deliberately.] Nothing cascades yet; the office withdraws each day by hand.
- **Telling the worker "all 3 days" up front.** [Yes: Invites, Shifts and Radar show the requirement before they Accept.] Slice 1 tells them only when a day stops them.
- **Per event rather than per role.** [Per role, as built.] A per-event switch would be a convenience that sets the flag on every section.
- **Declining a sibling day.** A worker who **declined** Saturday is not gated off Sunday (only a self-cancel is, via `self_cancelled`). [Treat a decline of a flagged day as a refusal of the job.]
- **Fixing a clash.** Turning the flag on for one day's role does not turn it on for the other days' roles; the office switches each day. A prompt that offers it is a small follow-up.

## Consequences

- **Supersedes ADR-0043 for sibling days.** ADR-0043 says the availability calendar never gates a manual invite, an Accept, an apply or a take. That still holds for the section's **own** day. For another day of a same-staff-all-days job it does not: a calendar entry there is `not_available_all_days` for every source, a manual invite and a worker's own Accept included (strict, by decision). The old comments are not edited (applied migrations); the migration appends a "superseded" note to the comments on `invite_worker`, `accept_invite`, `apply_to_shift`, `accept_application`, `take_offered_shift`, `staff_unavailable` and `staff_unavailability`, and ADR-0043 carries a pointer here. The office's way round is to switch the requirement off for that day.
- **Manual invites are gated too**, as asked: `invite_worker(..., 'manual')` is refused with `not_available_all_days`. This is stricter than the worker's own calendar on the section itself, where the office can "Invite anyway".
- **Existing invitations are not withdrawn** (§3.4), but an Accept on one is refused if a sibling day has since gone wrong, and the invitation is left live.
- **A day that has ended is not a sibling.** A worker who did not work Friday can still be invited to Sunday once Friday is over; slice 1 does not know who "was on" Friday.
- **Measured cost** (local PostgreSQL 16, 1,038 workers all holding the role; `authenticated` times out at 8 s):

  | | before (every caller read the wrapper) | after, one worker |
  |---|---|---|
  | 3-day flagged section, read the gate for one worker | 6.6 s | 0.02 s |
  | 5-day flagged section, same | 9.9 s (over the limit) | 0.02 s |
  | `invite_worker` + `accept_invite`, 3-day | ~7 s each | 0.02–0.03 s each |
  | `invite_worker` + `accept_invite`, 5-day | ~10 s each | 0.02–0.03 s each |
  | an unflagged section | the pool, ~1.5 s | the same row, ~0.02 s |

  pgTAP 804 pins the per-worker paths under 2 s on this data.
- **Known limit: the list path.** The board and the auto-assign rounds cost about (1 + number of siblings) pool runs, in proportion to the workers who hold the role: 6.9 s for a 3-day and 10.4 s for a 5-day job with 1,038 role-qualified workers locally. The auto-assign job runs as `service_role`, which Supabase gives no role-level 8 s limit by default, so the rounds are unaffected; the board opens as an `authenticated` office session and **can reach the 8 s limit for a job of five or more days with a very large pool**; hosted is slower than local. Sibling runs already skip workers the section's own gates have removed, so a pool where most workers lack the role is far cheaper. If it matters in practice the board should read the gate per displayed worker, or sibling runs should drop the five scoring inputs they do not use (about a quarter of a pool run).
- **Cover-request waves read the list too.** `offer_wave1_exhausted()` and `offer_candidates()` (an unqualified worker taking an offer; the offer pushes) read the wrapper, so on a flagged section they pay the list cost above. They run only for a cover request on a flagged section.
- **Radar's wave check is series-blind.** RULE-17 asks whether any worker qualified at the client and role is still reachable. `staff_open_shifts` evaluates that over the section's own pool, so a qualified worker the series gate has barred still counts as reachable and can keep an unqualified worker off the Radar for a flagged section until they are invited or the wave is exhausted. The auto-assign rounds (which read the list) are unaffected.
- **Redefining `auto_assign_candidates()` or one of the restated callers from an older body drops the gate.** A new pool gate goes in `auto_assign_candidates_pool()`. pgTAP 804 fails if the wrapper is replaced or a caller loses the gate.
- `series_id` and `same_staff_all_days` reach no `client_*` view and no client policy (ADR-0004, ADR-0026); pgTAP 804 asserts it. The pool, the new helpers and `link_event_series` are executable by `authenticated` and `service_role` only (never `anon` or PUBLIC); the pool keeps its `authenticated` grant because the invoker wrapper runs as the caller and the board calls it as an office session.
- **Wireframe deviation.** The "Day N of M" pill sits in the Event panel's header actions beside the status pill, where the page renders it; the wireframe draws it in the title row.
- **Not changed:** §3.4's waves, scoring and weights; check-in; pay; the Staff App's screens.
